# Useful evidence for a goalkeeper portfolio

Research date: 5 October 2026. This note answers [the goalkeeper evidence research question](../tasks/02-goalkeeper-portfolio-evidence.md). It supports discovery, not a finished build specification or a scouting assessment of this player.

## Recommendation

Give a visitor an accurate player profile, footage they can understand, and an appropriate route to ask about football opportunities. Use a short selection of goalkeeper actions for the first look, with links to longer match footage when available. The website can make existing evidence easier to find; the sources below do not establish that owning a website improves recruitment outcomes.

These are website recommendations informed by the sources, not universal scout requirements. We have not assessed the cousin's football ability, confirmed his age, or established his club or recruitment status.

## Translate that into material we can use

The suggestions in this section are our interpretation of the evidence above. Ask what already exists before requesting new filming or editing.

### Player facts

Start with his preferred public name, goalkeeper position, confirmed current club or training setting, age group, preferred foot, and a short dated playing history. Height in centimetres can be useful if measured and current, but omit it until confirmed. [Atlético de Madrid's official Jan Oblak profile](https://www.atleticodemadrid.com/jugadores/jan-oblak-2025-2026) illustrates a senior goalkeeper profile with position, height, birth date, nationality and club history. This is an example of presentation, not a reason to publish every field about a young player.

Ask the family which age information they want public. Age group or birth year may be enough for the public profile; exact birth date can remain a detail they provide directly if needed. Agree on this after we know his age and wishes. Do not ask for identity documents, medical records or contracts to build the website.

Separate playing for a club from attending its camp, training there, being invited to a trial, or discussing recruitment. Confirm the exact organization behind "Cali" and the current stage before writing copy. A visit or encounter with a national team player is not an endorsement. Ask what happened, how they know, and whether anyone has approved mentioning it.

### Goalkeeper footage

A reel should reflect the footage available. Useful candidate actions, based on the FIFA topics above, include:

- Saves with enough build-up to see his starting position and the attack, plus what happens to the ball afterward.
- One-on-one situations that show when he advances or holds his position.
- Crosses and balls into the area that show how he judges whether to claim, punch or stay.
- Through-balls that show his position relative to defenders and when he leaves the goal.
- Receiving and passing with his feet, offering support to defenders, and short or longer distribution under real match pressure.

These are examples to look for, not boxes the family must fill. If only saves are recorded, describe that selection honestly. Training footage can help explain practice and technique, but label it as training. Do not present a drill as competitive match evidence.

For each clip, retain enough play before and after the action to understand the decision and outcome. Use the original speed for the main view. A replay, if helpful, should be clearly identifiable. Heavy cropping and edits can hide teammates, opponents or the result of a pass.

A full match or an uninterrupted longer passage offers context that a chosen collection of successes cannot. That is our recommendation based on FIFA's treatment of the goalkeeper within the team and the FA's use of multiple observations. None of these sources says all scouts require a full match, and we found no universal preferred reel duration. Offer longer footage alongside the highlights if the family already has it and can share it. A selected reel should never claim to be a complete assessment.

### Dates, provenance and statistics

Keep a small internal record for each asset: original file or link, recorder or publisher, match date, team and opponent, competition and age group if known, whether it is training or a match, the player's shirt colour or number, useful timestamps, and whether permission to use it has been confirmed. These are content-management recommendations to make clips understandable and prevent accidental reuse of incorrect material. Unknown details should stay marked unknown.

Give public videos meaningful labels, such as a match date and opponent when confirmed. Keep original files if available; repeatedly forwarded copies may make the player and ball harder to see. Ask who recorded the footage and whom we should ask about using it. Do not assume that receiving a clip means the website may publish it.

Statistics are optional. Use only figures someone can explain and check, with the season or competition, period covered and source. Appearances or minutes may be enough if reliable records exist. Do not invent saves, clean sheets, rankings or percentages from an edited reel. We found no primary source here that makes advanced statistics necessary for this website.

### Contact

Ask who should receive football enquiries, their role, preferred channel, and what contact details they want public. If he is a minor, a parent or guardian may be the appropriate recipient. A real authorized representative is another option if one exists. Manchester City's form and the FA pathway above demonstrate parent involvement in their youth processes; they do not dictate this website's exact contact design.

My website recommendation is one monitored email address with a plain label such as "Football enquiries" and a clear recipient role. Confirm a public phone or WhatsApp link separately rather than assuming the family wants it. Social links can serve followers. Do not invent an agent, availability for transfer, or willingness to relocate. Request further match information through the agreed contact if a public schedule is unnecessary.

## Prioritized family questionnaire requests

This is a proposed material list for the questionnaire workflow, not a questionnaire that has been sent.

1. Confirm his wishes for the website and who approves public content. Ask his preferred public name, age or age group, confirmed current team or training setting, and what kinds of opportunity he wants. Invite feedback on prioritizing football opportunities while welcoming followers.
2. Confirm who will handle football enquiries and their preferred public contact. Clarify the recruitment story involving "Cali" and any notable encounter before considering either for public copy.
3. Ask for footage they already have, including highlights, longer matches or club links. For each, ask what match or training session it is, roughly when it happened, how to identify him, who recorded or published it, and whether we may use it. "Unknown" and "we can find out later" are valid answers.
4. Ask for a few photos they like, a recent action photo and a portrait if available, and who took them. Ask which social accounts are his and whether he wants them linked.
5. Ask for a short playing history, preferred foot, confirmed height if available, and any achievements they can describe accurately. Reliable stats and a coach's view of his strengths are optional extras. A coach quotation requires that person's agreement before publication.

The first questionnaire should ask for existing materials, not assign the family an expensive production task. We can decide what is usable after reviewing what they share. If there is no full match or professional photography, that alone should not block discovery or force a paid service.

## Limits and remaining gaps

- No public source reviewed here establishes a universal scout checklist, reel length, minimum height, required statistic, or guaranteed benefit from a personal website. Keep those claims out of project advice.
- The Colombian club examples are relevant public profile patterns. They do not establish how the cousin's actual club recruits. Once the family confirms the organization and situation, we can inspect its official guidance if that becomes useful.
- The accessible FIFA sources are coaching and analysis material. Their value is explaining what goalkeeper actions mean, rather than promising what a recruiter will choose.
- Fulham's official trials page appeared in search with CV fields, but the opened page returned only its heading and navigation. I have not used those indexed details as current requirements. FIFA's talent-identification guide PDF also failed to open, so it contributes no claims to this note.
- We have no player assets, measurements, coach feedback or family approvals to evaluate yet. The next evidence step is the questionnaire, followed by reviewing the actual footage and facts they can provide.
