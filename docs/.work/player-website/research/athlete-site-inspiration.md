# Athlete website inspiration

Observed on 5 October 2026. Research for [the inspiration ticket](../tasks/01-athlete-site-inspiration.md). This note informs the wayfinder session; it does not approve a design or set the build scope.

## Recommendation for discussion

Borrow Lando Norris's separation between sport and personality, and the strong photography and visual identity shared by both references. For this goalkeeper, give visitors immediate paths to footage, player information, his story, and the agreed contact. Let playful details express his own personality. The family should help choose those details.

These are design recommendations, not evidence that a website improves recruitment outcomes. Neither reference establishes how emerging footballers usually manage their websites, or what scouts require.

## What the references actually do

### Lando Norris

The [homepage](https://landonorris.com/) divides content into "On Track" and "Off Track", connects visitors to a calendar, helmet collection, store, partnerships, social accounts, and a business contact. A photo-led personal story gives fans more than race results. The desktop view has a large portrait, a pale background, lime accents, curved outlines, and a helmet-like mesh over the portrait. These are direct page and screenshot observations.

The [On Track page](https://landonorris.com/on-track) presents career statistics, results, junior-career milestones, and the schedule. The separate [Off Track page](https://landonorris.com/off-track) gives personal and promotional activity its own destination. That split makes room for different visitor interests without giving each audience a separate website.

Inference for our project: "Football" and "My story" could provide a similar split with more familiar labels in Spanish. Football visitors need footage and confirmed facts near the beginning. Followers can explore his story and approved social links without getting in the way of that route. This is a proposed structure to discuss, not a claim about scout behavior.

### Max Verstappen

The current [landing page](https://www.verstappen.com/) puts merchandise and tickets in the hero. Further sections lead to news, racing activities, and race tickets. The desktop screenshot shows a large portrait, oversized white MV mark, dark blue background, red buttons, and bold lettering. The mark dominates the first view. These are direct observations of the current root page, rather than an older version of the site.

The wider [official platform](https://www.verstappen.com/home/) also advertises news, social links, and newsletter registration. Its [join-us page](https://www.verstappen.com/join-us) offers a customer-service route. That contact supports commerce; it is not a template for handling player opportunities.

Inference for our project: use strong photography, consistent colors, and obvious next actions. Replace celebrity commerce priorities with watching football, reading the profile, and making an opportunity enquiry. A personal logo is optional; his name and good photographs can establish identity without waiting for a logo.

## Practical translation to this project

The table below is our judgment about scope and upkeep. It does not report either athlete's budget, staffing, or backend.

| Idea | Possible use here | Maintenance implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sport and personal content have distinct paths | Football information and footage first; story and social links easy to find alongside them | Occasional edits can serve both audiences. Avoid needing a constant news feed. |
| A recognizable photographic identity | An approved portrait plus a strong goalkeeper action photo, with a consistent accent color | Needs suitable originals and permission to use them. Keep images replaceable. |
| Playful personal details | His own words, an approved nickname, a small glove or pitch motif, a personal photo caption | Mostly one-time work. Ask him which details feel like him. |
| Motion and interactive visuals | Brief optional transitions or a small decorative interaction | Each added interaction needs device, keyboard, and reduced-motion checks. A large animated entrance should not delay access to footage or contact. |
| Career history and recent achievements | A short dated timeline of confirmed milestones | Review after a team change or important event; update both languages together. |
| Social accounts | Links to accounts the family approves | Lower upkeep than recreating every social update on the website. A live feed introduces another dependency. |
| Contact for a specific purpose | One agreed contact for football opportunities | Someone must monitor it. Choose the person and channel with the family before publishing. |
| Store, ticketing, partner campaigns, newsletter | Future options only if a real need and someone to operate them emerge | Each creates a continuing publishing or operational job. The references' existence does not justify adding them now. |

For Spanish and English, draft and approve the Spanish content first, then translate the same facts and captions. A visible language switch should preserve the visitor's place where practical. No bilingual behavior was audited on these references; this recommendation follows the owner's stated language priorities.

For footage, propose a short highlight video with labeled links to longer match footage. The label can identify the date, competition, and opponents when confirmed. The inspected pages are not goalkeeper recruitment examples, so this is a project proposal rather than a pattern proved by these sites. Ask a coach or informed family contact which clips best represent him, including useful play beyond spectacular saves.

Leonardo can handle edits initially. Before a future dashboard or marketing handoff, keep content and asset information organized: Spanish text, English text, media originals, captions, dates, permissions, and the person who approves changes. A dashboard can be chosen later when we know who will use it and what they update. This is a maintenance recommendation, not an architectural decision.

## What remains to ask the family

- Which audiences and next actions matter most to him? Does he like football opportunities first with room for fans and his story?
- Which photos, footage, social accounts, and personal details can we publish? Who owns or can authorize each asset?
- Which facts and milestones are confirmed, and who approves them? In particular, do not turn the rough Cali recruitment account into public copy without confirmation.
- What does he like about the two references: photographs, colors, personality, layout, or interaction? Which elements would feel wrong for him?
- Who should receive enquiries and who approves later updates? His age also affects the family discussions about what information to publish.

## Inspection limits

Web text and desktop browser screenshots informed this note. Lando's menu was opened and its sport/personality destinations were read. Max's cookie overlay was dismissed to inspect the hero. No accounts, forms, purchases, or external messages were submitted. Mobile behavior, load speed, video playback, accessibility, and the complete motion behavior were not audited. Their technology choices, hosting prices, staffing, and conversion performance were not established. Asset-heavy or interactive design suggests extra work for our project; it does not prove either site's costs or usability.
